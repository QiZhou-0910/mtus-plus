14:T53a,<p>The Multinational Time Use Study (MTUS) is the world&#39;s largest harmonised
collection of time-use diary data. It brings national time-use surveys from
dozens of countries and several decades into one common format. Diary
structures, activity classifications and key background variables are
standardised. Researchers can then study how people divide their day between
paid work, unpaid work, care, leisure and sleep, within and across countries
and over time.</p>
<p>MTUS+ is the largest redesign of the MTUS framework in many years. There
are two reasons for it:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>More diverse surveys.</strong> MTUS has traditionally drawn on surveys from
high-income countries. It now includes more surveys from low- and
middle-income countries (LMICs). Many of these use different activity
classifications, diary layouts and contextual questions. MTUS+ uses a more
flexible framework that keeps these differences and still supports
comparison.</li>
<li><strong>Keeping more information.</strong> Earlier MTUS releases focused on variables
that could be harmonised across as many surveys as possible. Information
that only some surveys collected was often lost. MTUS+ keeps much more of
the original survey content alongside the harmonised variables.</li>
</ul>
<p>The data are free of charge for non-commercial research.</p>

identifiers are preserved. The names of the source identifier variables are
recorded so records can be linked back to the original microdata.

25:T583,<p>MTUS began in the mid-1980s. Jonathan Gershuny, working with Sally Jones at
the University of Bath, set out to update and extend the comparative
time-use data that Alexander Szalai had assembled in the 1960s. Since then,
