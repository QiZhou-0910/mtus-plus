What Is MTUS+?

The Multinational Time Use Study (MTUS) is the world's largest harmonised collection of time-use diary data. It brings national time-use surveys from dozens of countries and several decades into one common format. Diary structures, activity classifications and key background variables are standardised. Researchers can then study how people divide their day between paid work, unpaid work, care, leisure and sleep, within and across countries and over time.

MTUS+ is the largest redesign of the MTUS framework in many years. There are two reasons for it:

  • More diverse surveys. MTUS has traditionally drawn on surveys from high-income countries. It now includes more surveys from low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Many of these use different activity classifications, diary layouts and contextual questions. MTUS+ uses a more flexible framework that keeps these differences and still supports comparison.
  • Keeping more information. Earlier MTUS releases focused on variables that could be harmonised across as many surveys as possible. Information that only some surveys collected was often lost. MTUS+ keeps much more of the original survey content alongside the harmonised variables.

The data are free of charge for non-commercial research.

Existing users can carry on with legacy MTUS, and both product lines will continue to be supported. See Data files for details.

What's New In MTUS+

  • One integrated file. Episodes, people, households, diaries and survey characteristics are all in a single episode-level file, so separate files no longer have to be merged.
  • Several activity classifications. ICATUS 2016 and HETUS 2018 are available at 1-, 2- and 3-digit detail, alongside the CTUR 69-activity scheme used in legacy MTUS.
  • Up to four simultaneous activities. There are tertiary and quaternary activity layers as well as primary and secondary. This matters for wide-interval diaries, which allow several activities per time slot.
  • Original diary fields kept. Up to twelve original episode-level fields (OF1–OF12) are stored as text, so every harmonisation decision can be inspected.
  • Episodes follow the original diary. Episode boundaries are not collapsed to fit one harmonised scheme. Users can redefine episodes for their own analysis.
  • Survey-level metadata. A family of SVY* variables records diary design, fieldwork period, interval length, collection mode, information available, and how well each activity classification can be represented.
  • Household structure from the roster. New variables describe generations, household structure, each person's role in the household and the ages of several children.
  • New diary and background information. Activity purpose (common in LMIC surveys), affect or enjoyment (some high-income surveys), religion, a broader ethnic or social-group variable, household expenditure and the education of the household head. The sex/gender variable gains an "other / non-binary" category.
  • Survey design and weights. PSU and STRATA are included for design-based standard errors. A new weight, PROPWT_ALL, keeps all diaries.
  • Traceable identifiers. Original household, person and diary identifiers are preserved. The names of the source identifier variables are recorded so records can be linked back to the original microdata. To be confirmed: the User Guide and Codebook say hldid, persid and id are strings. The current harmonisation files store them as numbers. Which is final?

Phased Harmonisation

Re-harmonising every historical survey from its original microdata before release would delay MTUS+ considerably. The transition is therefore phased:

  • Fully harmonised surveys (SVYPHASE = 1). These are newly added surveys, plus selected earlier surveys that have been re-harmonised from the original microdata under the full MTUS+ protocol.
  • Legacy surveys under interim harmonisation (SVYPHASE = 0). These are surveys from earlier MTUS releases converted into the MTUS+ structure. Their ICATUS and HETUS variables are derived from the MTUS 69-activity scheme through documented crosswalks. They keep the legacy episode structure.

Legacy surveys will be re-harmonised progressively in future releases.

History And Partnerships

MTUS began in the mid-1980s. Jonathan Gershuny, working with Sally Jones at the University of Bath, set out to update and extend the comparative time-use data that Alexander Szalai had assembled in the 1960s. Since then, MTUS has grown in geographical coverage and detail, adding episode-level data, major comparative programmes and many national surveys.

  • IPUMS. The Centre for Time Use Research (CTUR) works with the IPUMS Time Use team at the University of Minnesota and the Maryland Population Research Center. CTUR produces the harmonised files. IPUMS runs the IPUMS MTUS online extract system.
  • Child Penalty Atlas. A collaboration with the Child Penalty Atlas team at the LSE, led by Camille Landais, has sped up the expansion of MTUS. Members of that team are now part of the core MTUS authorship. To be confirmed: Camille Landais to check this paragraph
  • China Time Use Survey. MTUS works with Wenbin Wang and Fenglian Du (Inner Mongolia University) and the wider China Time Use Survey team. The aim is to open routes for international comparative research with Chinese time-use data. To be confirmed: Wenbin Wang to check this paragraph

Every effort has been made to keep the data and documentation accurate, but errors and ambiguities can remain. Please report problems or questions to ctur@ucl.ac.uk. User feedback has always been central to improving MTUS.
