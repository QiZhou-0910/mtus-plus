<li><strong>Conservative harmonisation.</strong> Source information is mapped onto a
harmonised variable only when the correspondence is clear. When in doubt,
the harmonised value is left missing rather than forced into a category.
MTUS+ does not &quot;correct&quot; source data because a value looks implausible.</li>
<li><strong>Preserving user choice.</strong> Where several reasonable treatments exist,
MTUS+ keeps the information users need to choose for themselves, rather
than building one decision irreversibly into the data.</li>
<li><strong>User engagement and continuous improvement.</strong> MTUS+ is an evolving
harmonisation. Decisions are revisited when documentation improves, when
data producers clarify things, or when users report problems. Changes are
reflected in updated documentation and syntax.</li>
<li><strong>Transparency, reproducibility and traceability.</strong> Users should be able to
see how the data were built, where each survey came from, and the exact
transformations applied.</li>

17:T737,<p><strong>Step 0. Data acquisition and initial assessment.</strong> CTUR asks the national
statistical office or other producer for the microdata, and for permission to
redistribute the harmonised version free of charge for non-commercial
research. Original files are backed up and kept separate from working files.
Where an agreement requires it, CTUR deletes its copies, including backups,
once harmonisation is complete. The harmoniser then studies the data and
documentation together.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1. Restructuring the diary and building the master file.</strong> The diary
is converted into the MTUS+ episode format, whatever its original layout:
episode format, calendar (time-slot) format, or wide-interval diaries that
record several activities per slot. Timing is standardised and checked. The
episode file is then merged with the household, person and other source files
into one master file.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2. Creating harmonised variables.</strong> Source information is mapped onto
the MTUS+ variable definitions and coding schemes. This includes survey-level
variables, household structure derived from the roster, activity
classifications and other diary fields. Original diary fields are kept
alongside.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3. Quality control and output.</strong> Standard programs check:</p>
<ul>
<li><em>structure</em>: the expected variables are present, in the right format, with
correct missing-value codes and valid values only;</li>
<li><em>substance</em>: distributions of background variables, the time-use
distribution and the timing of activities through the day.</li>
</ul>
<p>Further survey metadata is then generated. A small number of deterministic
imputations fill episode information that other diary fields clearly imply.
The episode structure is checked once more before the files are produced.</p>
