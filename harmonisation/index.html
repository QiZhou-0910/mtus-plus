Harmonisation

From original survey to MTUS+, step by step.

  1. 0AcquireMicrodata and permission to redistribute.
  2. 1RestructureAny diary layout, turned into episodes.
  3. 2HarmoniseCommon variables and classifications.
  4. 3CheckStructural and substantive quality checks.

Principles

  • Conservative harmonisation. Source information is mapped onto a harmonised variable only when the correspondence is clear. When in doubt, the harmonised value is left missing rather than forced into a category. MTUS+ does not "correct" source data because a value looks implausible.
  • Preserving user choice. Where several reasonable treatments exist, MTUS+ keeps the information users need to choose for themselves, rather than building one decision irreversibly into the data.
  • User engagement and continuous improvement. MTUS+ is an evolving harmonisation. Decisions are revisited when documentation improves, when data producers clarify things, or when users report problems. Changes are reflected in updated documentation and syntax.
  • Transparency, reproducibility and traceability. Users should be able to see how the data were built, where each survey came from, and the exact transformations applied.

The Four-Step Protocol

Step 0. Data acquisition and initial assessment. CTUR asks the national statistical office or other producer for the microdata, and for permission to redistribute the harmonised version free of charge for non-commercial research. Original files are backed up and kept separate from working files. Where an agreement requires it, CTUR deletes its copies, including backups, once harmonisation is complete. The harmoniser then studies the data and documentation together.

Step 1. Restructuring the diary and building the master file. The diary is converted into the MTUS+ episode format, whatever its original layout: episode format, calendar (time-slot) format, or wide-interval diaries that record several activities per slot. Timing is standardised and checked. The episode file is then merged with the household, person and other source files into one master file.

Step 2. Creating harmonised variables. Source information is mapped onto the MTUS+ variable definitions and coding schemes. This includes survey-level variables, household structure derived from the roster, activity classifications and other diary fields. Original diary fields are kept alongside.

Step 3. Quality control and output. Standard programs check:

  • structure: the expected variables are present, in the right format, with correct missing-value codes and valid values only;
  • substance: distributions of background variables, the time-use distribution and the timing of activities through the day.

Further survey metadata is then generated. A small number of deterministic imputations fill episode information that other diary fields clearly imply. The episode structure is checked once more before the files are produced.

Human Review

Each survey is harmonised by a designated team member, who can consult the most senior team member at any point. A second team member then reviews the data and syntax. Finally the most senior team member reviews it and resolves remaining questions of interpretation. This three-person review complements the automated checks. To be confirmed: whether the three-stage review covers Steps 1 and 2 only. The draft has an open note on this.

Use Of Artificial Intelligence

AI is used as an auxiliary tool: to help interpret and translate source documentation, to identify relevant variables, and to help draft Stata syntax. The harmoniser supervises all AI-assisted work and checks every suggestion against the original documentation and microdata before using it. All harmonisation decisions remain the team's responsibility. AI is never used to generate or impute survey observations.

Harmonisation Syntax

Every harmonised survey has a Stata do-file recording the complete sequence of transformations, from original microdata to final output, including survey-specific decisions. Do-files will be published progressively, starting with newly harmonised surveys. Users with access to the original microdata can use them to reproduce the harmonisation.
