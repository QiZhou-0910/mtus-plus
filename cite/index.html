How To Cite

Cite the release you used.

A Simple Rule

  • Using MTUS+ data: cite the MTUS+ data release you used.
  • Using a specialised MTUS+ collection: cite that collection and the overall MTUS+ release, as its documentation specifies.
  • Reproducing, adapting or building on survey-specific harmonisation code: cite the relevant harmonisation syntax or publication.
  • Using a specific method, crosswalk or algorithm on its own: cite the corresponding methodological or code resource.

You do not need to cite each country's harmonisation file just because that country is in your analysis.

Three Levels Of Citation

  1. The MTUS collection as a whole. Use this when referring to MTUS as a long-running research infrastructure.
  2. The two product lines (MTUS and MTUS+). Use these when referring to either framework in general.
  3. Individual data releases, such as MTUS+ Version 1. Always use these for empirical work, because they identify the exact data used.

When To Cite Harmonisation Syntax As Well

Survey-specific harmonisation syntax is published as a citable research output. It credits the researchers who did the harmonisation and makes the process transparent. Cite it in addition to the data release when:

  • your work focuses on one or a few surveys and relies substantially on how they were reconstructed, classified or transformed;
  • you reproduce the MTUS+ harmonisation from the original source data;
  • you adapt MTUS+ syntax to harmonise another survey or wave;
  • you use a specific transformation on its own, such as an activity crosswalk, the treatment of simultaneity, the household-structure algorithm, timing reconstruction or a weighting procedure;
  • you study harmonisation methods, comparability or data construction.

Each published syntax package will name its contributors and carry a persistent identifier and recommended citation.

Citing Documentation

Cite the relevant MTUS+ document when you use or discuss its definitions, procedures or guidance. This applies to the User Guide, Codebook, Harmonisation and Quality Report, Transition Guide, Source Inventory and published harmonisation code. China Time Use Surveys

The China Time Use Surveys are not distributed as part of MTUS+. Cite both the original survey and the MTUS harmonisation syntax used to convert it.

Du, F., and Wang, W. (2017). Chinese Time Use Survey 2017. Survey and Research Center for Chinese Time Use, Inner Mongolia University.

Du, F., and Wang, W. (2021). Chinese Time Use Survey 2021. Survey and Research Center for Chinese Time Use, Inner Mongolia University.

Du, F., and Wang, W. (2025). Chinese Time Use Survey 2025. Survey and Research Center for Chinese Time Use, Inner Mongolia University.
