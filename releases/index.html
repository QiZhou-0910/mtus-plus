MTUS+ Version 1

The first public release of MTUS+. To be confirmed: release date (planned for late October 2026)

Release 1 contains:

  • the MTUS+ Integrated File (and possibly country or regional subsets);
  • surveys fully harmonised under the MTUS+ protocol;
  • legacy MTUS surveys converted to the MTUS+ structure under interim harmonisation;
  • a parallel release of legacy MTUS files.

To be confirmed: the definitive Release 1 survey list, with each survey marked as fully harmonised or transitional

To be confirmed: the file list, formats and sizes for the release notes

Changes From Legacy MTUS

  • One Integrated File replaces the separate HAF and HEF in MTUS+. The legacy files continue in parallel.
  • New activity classifications: ICATUS 2016 and HETUS 2018 at 1-, 2- and 3-digit level, alongside CTUR 69. The 25-activity scheme stays in the legacy files only.
  • Up to four activity layers (pri_, sec_, ter_, quat_) instead of primary and secondary only.
  • Episodes follow the original diary in fully harmonised surveys.
  • Original diary fields of1–of12, plus purpose and affect, kept as text.
  • Survey-level variables (svy*) describing diary design, fieldwork and classification coverage.
  • Household structure from the roster: ngens, gen, hhstruct, hhrole, agech1–agech4 and more.
  • New background variables: religion, hexpend, household assets and energy sources; ethnic widened to other social-group classifications.
  • Sex: a third category, other / non-binary.
  • Weights and design: new propwt_all; psu and strata where available.
  • Identifiers: original identifiers kept, with hldid_name, persid_name and id_name naming the source variables.
  • Missing activity: system missing in ICATUS and HETUS; code 69 kept in CTUR 69.

To be confirmed: confirm this list against the final Version 1 files; the BADCASE coding may also change

Known Limitations

  • Interim activity codes for legacy surveys. ICATUS and HETUS variables are derived from the legacy 69-activity scheme, not from the original national codes. Detailed ICATUS categories for employment and own-use production often cannot be reconstructed.
  • Two episode definitions. Fully harmonised surveys keep the original diary's episodes; legacy surveys keep the legacy definition. Do not compare episode counts or durations across the two without redefining episodes.
  • Non-harmonised text variables in legacy surveys may hold numeric codes rather than descriptive labels.
  • Household roster variables are only available where a roster exists. They will be added progressively as national statistical offices supply rosters.
  • Replicate-weight variance designs are not yet harmonised.

To be confirmed: other known limitations from the Release 1 quality checks

Planned Developments

  • Re-harmonising legacy surveys from the original microdata, starting with surveys whose original data and activity coding allow accurate reconstruction.
  • More surveys, particularly from Africa, Asia and Latin America, as harmonisation and redistribution permissions are completed.
  • Harmonisation syntax for more surveys, and R equivalents of the epigenx and timealloc tools.
  • MTUS Special Collections. Small, linkable sets of derived variables for particular research areas. Planned examples are health (sleep timing, physical-activity intensity from MET values) and environment (energy use and carbon coefficients for everyday activities). To be confirmed: project title and grant number funding the Special Collections
  • MTUS Kids. Adding parental and household information to children's diary records, where technically and legally feasible.
  • Commuting and transport. Harmonising, and where justified imputing, travel episodes from location sequences, episode order and transport codes.
  • Stylised (non-diary) time-use surveys. Possible inclusion, clearly flagged and kept analytically separate, to extend coverage and support validation studies. To be confirmed: whether this is a decided plan; the User Guide marks it "to be decided"
  • Harmonised purpose and affect indicators, once enough comparable surveys exist.
